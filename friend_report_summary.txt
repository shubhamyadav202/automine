1. ABSTRACT
Severe air pollution is one of the most critical public health hazards facing urban populations,
yet individual response mechanisms remain predominantly reactive, fragmented, and limited
to passive air quality index (AQI) readings. VayuRakshak AI is an intelligent, privacy-
preserving air protection and proactive health advisory platform designed to bridge the gap
between atmospheric hazard monitoring and everyday preventive action under the ethos “We
don’t just inform. We protect.” The platform connects users with real-time hyperlocal air
quality data, regional satellite fire signals (NASA FIRMS integration points), and 72-hour
pollution forecasts to deliver actionable exposure-mitigation strategies.
The system empowers users through an explainable Indoor Protection Risk Score, intelligent
commute exposure routing (identifying cleaner alternatives with up to 28%–43% estimated
exposure reduction and delayed-departure planning), and a smart Calendar Guard that
proactively reschedules outdoor activities away from toxic peak windows. To safeguard
indoor environments, VayuRakshak incorporates an automated Protection Autopilot that
generates approval-bound air purifier workflows with transparent audit logs and manual
override controls. In addition, an integrated conversational assistant—Vayu Guide—provides
grounded local retrieval (RAG) over curated health and atmospheric documentation,
supplemented by multi-modal speech narration via Edge TTS and guided interface
walkthroughs.
The prototype is built on a modern full-stack web architecture featuring a Next.js
16 and React 19 frontend, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, and a modular Node.js backend
managed via npm workspaces. Security and privacy are enforced through authenticated server
sessions, salted scrypt password hashing, HttpOnly cookies, and strict local evaluation
boundaries ensuring user telemetry and preferences remain strictly user-controlled without
unauthorized third-party leakage. VayuRakshak demonstrates how a low-overhead, human-in-
the-loop software platform can transform passive environmental awareness into explainable,
automated, and accountable personal air defense.

3. Introduction
Urban agglomerations and metropolitan regions across India face hazardous surges in ambient
air pollution, especially during the post-monsoon and winter seasons. According to
environmental monitoring records from the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) and state
pollution control boards across the National Capital Region (NCR) and major metropolitan
belts, particulate matter concentrations ($PM_{2.5}$ and $PM_{10}$) routinely spike past
250–400 $\mu g/m^3$, entering the “Very Poor” and “Severe” Air Quality Index (AQI)
categories. Managing and mitigating exposure across dense urban zones demands proactive
coordination between environmental monitoring networks, everyday commuters, residential
environments, and civic health advisories.
Citizens currently monitor air quality through multiple independent portals and applications,
including the CPCB Sameer portal, SAFAR-India, the national Air Quality Early Warning
System (AQEWS), and global commercial weather services. While having multiple monitoring
channels provides raw data, these public platforms primarily serve as retrospective notification
boards. They deliver static index numbers without explaining specific indoor vulnerability,
calculating personalized commute exposure, or guiding timely preventive action. For an
everyday urban resident, the core challenge is not simply observing an AQI number of 286, but
knowing what specific travel routes to take, when to delay outdoor exposure, and how to govern
indoor living spaces safely.
Modern software systems can bridge this gap between passive environmental telemetry and
actionable, personal protection without requiring costly or proprietary sensor hardware. A
centralized web application that evaluates exposure deltas along transit corridors, computes an
explainable indoor risk metric, coordinates calendar schedules with pollution forecasting, and
automates indoor purifier policies with strict user consent can transform passive awareness into
proactive defense. Such a system also generates localized insights, including peak exposure
windows, cleaner route comparison benchmarks, and regional fire hotspot signals (e.g., via
NASA FIRMS) that aid in everyday decision-making.
The VayuRakshak AI platform presented in this report is an end-to-end prototype of such an air
protection and proactive health defense system. Operating under the guiding philosophy “We
don’t just inform. We protect,” it features an integrated suite of modules including the

5. Existing Systems
1. Government Air Quality Portals & Mobile Apps (e.g., CPCB Sameer, SAFAR-India,
AQEWS)
The Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) and SAFAR operate official portals and the
Sameer mobile application to report National Air Quality Index (NAQI) figures from
continuous ambient air quality monitoring stations (CAAQMS). They categorize pollution
levels into standard bands (Good to Severe) and issue broad public health advisories.
Cons:
• Purely Passive & Retrospective: They function primarily as digital notice boards,
displaying past or current station averages without forward-looking, personalized
mitigation advice.
• No Commute or Transit Guidance: They do not correlate travel corridors with pollution
hot spots or suggest cleaner alternatives and delayed-departure windows for daily
commuters.
• Disconnected from Indoor Spaces: No guidance or mechanism is provided to assess
how ambient smog infiltrates homes or how indoor air purifiers should be operated.

2. Commercial Weather Apps & IoT Air Monitors (e.g., IQAir, Plume Labs,
AccuWeather)
Commercial services combine satellite observations with ground stations, and proprietary IoT
air monitors (e.g., consumer PM2.5 monitors) allow users to monitor ambient and room air
quality on mobile dashboards.
Cons:
• Hardware-Dependent & Cost-Prohibitive: Effective indoor protection typically requires
purchasing expensive, proprietary sensors and smart air purifiers for every room.
• Opaque Risk Metrics: They offer generic ratings without an explainable breakdown of
risk factors (e.g., duration of exposure, outdoor infiltration, room volume, or individual
sensitivity).
• Black-Box Automation: Connected smart appliances either run continuously (wasting
energy and filter life) or act autonomously without transparent user approval, audit
trails, or clear cancellation controls.
3. Standard Map & Navigation Systems (e.g., Google Maps, Apple Maps)
Everyday commuters rely on commercial navigation applications to plan travel across urban
regions. These tools optimize routes dynamically based on real-time traffic congestion, tolls,
and physical travel distance.
Cons:
• Zero Exposure Optimization: They route commuters exclusively for speed or distance,
frequently funneling pedestrians, two-wheeler riders, and car commuters directly
through severe pollution hot spots and stagnant traffic choke points.
• No Temporal Exposure Planning: They do not evaluate exposure deltas across
departure windows (e.g., delaying a trip by 30 minutes to bypass a morning AQI
inversion peak).
• Lack of Integrated Calendar Protection: They do not sync with personal schedules to
flag or reschedule outdoor workouts and travel away from hazardous atmospheric
conditions.

6. Proposed Solution 1
IoT Dedicated Hardware Sensor & Smart Purifier Appliance Mesh:
In this approach, personal low-cost particulate matter sensor nodes ($PM_{2.5}$ / $PM_{10}$,
optical dust sensors like PMS5003 connected to an ESP32 microcontroller) are deployed in
every room and carried by commuters as wearable monitors. These hardware units continuously
stream real-time air quality metrics over Wi-Fi or cellular networks to a custom cloud IoT hub
(such as AWS IoT Core or MQTT broker). Connected smart air purifiers are hard-wired or
interfaced via custom relays to trigger instantly whenever indoor particulate thresholds are
breached.
This approach offers real-time physical readings directly at the user’s exact coordinate and
automates room purification without relying on regional atmospheric interpolations. However,
deploying custom hardware nodes requires substantial upfront capital expenditure, battery
management, ongoing calibration, and sensor maintenance. In outdoor and transit scenarios,
optical sensor optics degrade quickly due to dust and moisture. Furthermore, demonstrating a
physical hardware mesh across multiple commuter corridors and room settings is cost-
prohibitive and impractical for a student semester timeline, while still failing to provide
intelligent calendar planning or grounded health advisory support.
Cons:
1. High hardware cost: Requires microcontrollers, particulate sensors, enclosure
fabrication, and power supplies for every monitored room and wearable.
2. Sensor calibration & degradation: Optical dust sensors suffer from drift, calibration
errors in varying humidity, and physical fouling in high-smog conditions.
3. Hardware deployment overhead: Impractical to scale, deploy, and benchmark across
citywide commuter corridors within a semester project timeline.
4. Network and battery constraints: Wearable and outdoor sensor nodes require continuous
battery management and uninterrupted cellular/Wi-Fi telemetry.

7. Proposed Solution 2
Native Mobile App with Continuous GPS Telemetry & Cloud Push Notifications:
This approach provides a native mobile-first application (built on Android/iOS via React Native
or Flutter) that continuously tracks the user's real-time background GPS coordinates. The app
polls live traffic and environmental APIs in the background, calculates on-the-go exposure
during commutes, and sends aggressive push notifications or SMS alerts when the user
approaches an active pollution hotspot or enters a peak AQI zone.
Continuous location telemetry allows automatic detection of commuter movement without
requiring manual route lookups, and native system push notifications provide immediate alerts.
However, running continuous background GPS tracking introduces heavy battery drain and
raises severe user privacy and location-surveillance concerns. In addition, developing,
maintaining, and publishing native applications across multiple mobile OS ecosystems involves
longer release cycles, app-store overhead, and recurring costs for cloud push/SMS gateways.
More importantly, it shifts focus onto intrusive alerting rather than an explainable, controllable,
privacy-preserving desktop and responsive web interface where users can review detailed audit
logs, explore forecast models, and manage automation approvals. For these reasons, continuous
background GPS tracking and dedicated native apps are preserved as future scope.
Cons:
1. Severe privacy and surveillance risks: Continuous background GPS tracking exposes
personal movement telemetry to potential privacy leaks.
2. Battery and device drain: Persistent background geolocation and polling severely
deplete mobile device battery life.
3. Higher development overhead: Requires cross-platform mobile development
(Android/iOS) alongside cloud push notification pipelines (Firebase Cloud
Messaging/APNs), adding development time and recurring gateway expenses.
4. App store barriers: Requires users to download, permission-grant, and repeatedly
update mobile application binaries.
5. Intrusive alerting over proactive control: Tends to produce notification fatigue rather
than providing an explainable, auditable dashboard for route comparison and human-
in-the-loop home automation.

8. Proposed Recommended Solution
VayuRakshak AI — Intelligent Web-Based Air Protection & Human-in-the-Loop
Advisory System:
The recommended solution is a centralized, privacy-preserving web platform that bridges
environmental forecasting, transit exposure reduction, indoor air risk governance, and
grounded conversational health advisories under the ethos “We don’t just inform. We
protect.” A modular Node.js backend serves structured environmental contracts, exposure
calculations, and automation state, while a modern Next.js 16 / React 19 browser frontend
provides an accessible, responsive dashboard across desktops, tablets, and smartphones
without requiring any app installations.
Rather than relying on expensive hardware deployments or invasive mobile background GPS
tracking, VayuRakshak AI utilizes standard web APIs and curated atmospheric fixtures to
deliver tangible protections. Commuters can evaluate routes side-by-side to choose lower-
exposure corridors (achieving up to 28%–43% estimated exposure reduction) and leverage
delayed-departure scenarios to avoid peak morning pollution spikes. For indoor spaces, the
platform calculates an explainable Indoor Protection Risk Score (0–100) and operates
a Protection Autopilot that prepares pre-emptive air purifier schedules with explicit approval
workflows ("Allow once"), cancellation controls, and immutable audit logs. The system also
integrates Calendar Guard to reschedule high-risk outdoor activities and features Vayu Guide,
a local retrieval-augmented (RAG) assistant with voice narration (Edge TTS) that delivers
source-grounded respiratory health advice.
Pros:
1. Feasible & Low-Cost: Operates as a pure software web architecture needing no
proprietary sensor hardware or expensive cloud appliances; can be built, tested, and
demonstrated reliably within a semester project cycle.
2. Complete Protective Workflow: Bridges the entire life cycle of air defense—from 72-
hour peak forecasting and regional fire tracking (NASA FIRMS) to commute route
comparison, calendar adaptation, and indoor automation.
3. Explainability & Human-in-the-Loop: Risk scores (74/100 indoor risk baseline) are
fully broken down by quantifiable factors; automation runs strictly under user consent
with auditable logs, single-click approvals, and pause/cancellation controls.

VayuRakshak AI Architecture:
The system follows a modular 3-layer (Three-Tier) architecture, separating presentation,
application logic, and data persistence for heightened security, modularity, and maintainability:
1. Presentation Layer (Frontend):
• Built with Next.js 16 and React 19 using TypeScript and Tailwind CSS.
• Provides responsive user interfaces including the Tomorrow's Air
Plan dashboard, Route Exposure Comparison, Calendar Guard, Protection
Autopilot, and Vayu Guide.
• Includes interactive visualization components such as a lightweight WebGL/CSS
Atmospheric Globe and geospatial pollution maps.
• Operates in any standard browser across laptops and mobile devices without
requiring app installation.
2. Client-Server Communication:

• The frontend communicates via the browser's fetch API using JSON payloads
through same-origin Next.js API routes (/api), which forward requests to the core

• User authentication state is maintained securely using HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax

• Every automation action (e.g., triggering a purifier schedule) strictly enforces
consent transitions (PROPOSED → APPROVED / REJECTED →

1. Data Model (The Foundation)
The system is built on TypeScript interfaces and schema contracts shared across both services
(in shared/src/types/ and shared/src/data/):
• Core
Entities: User, Conditions, ForecastPoint, RouteOption, CalendarEvent, AutomationR
ule, ApprovalRecord, and HealthSummary.
• Enumerations & Strict Types: Type safety is guaranteed at compile time through fixed
union types:
• AQICategory: "Good" | "Moderate" | "Poor" | "Very Poor" | "Severe"
• TravelMode: "Walking" | "Cycling" | "Two-wheeler" | "Car" | "Metro"
• AutopilotScope: "once" | "always"
• AutomationStatus: "proposed" | "scheduled-demo" | "completed" | "cancelled"
• The Route & Risk Models: The route model calculates distance, duration, and
exposure delta against the fastest route baseline. The indoor protection model stores
ambient AQI, particulate penetration factor ($PM_{2.5}$ infiltration), room volume,
and filtration efficiency to generate an explainable 0–100 risk score (74/100 baseline).
2. The Repository & Storage Layer (Data Access)
• Atomic Persistent Account Store: User accounts are managed via an atomic file-
locked storage engine (accounts.json) with dedicated concurrency protection in the
runtime environment.
• Envelope Contracts & Fixture State: Data access is abstracted through envelope
patterns (DataEnvelope<T>), tagging each record with metadata (data, source: "demo"
| "live" | "cached", fetchedAt, and confidence).
• Grounded Document Index: Curated markdown documents
in frontend/content/*.md are pre-indexed into chunked JSON structures (npm run
index:content), enabling ultra-fast local keyword ranking and retrieval without
requiring expensive third-party vector databases.

5. Security & Session-Based RBAC
• Cryptographic Hashing: User passwords are encrypted using salted scrypt hashing
before persistence; plaintext passwords are never stored or logged.
• Session Protection: Upon login, an opaque random session token is issued, and its
SHA-256 digest is stored on the server. The client receives
an HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax, and Secure cookie, mitigating cross-site scripting (XSS)
and cross-site request forgery (CSRF).
• Protection Boundaries & Privacy: Personal schedules, purifier approvals, and chat
inquiries are resolved within the local application boundary; no sensitive user data is
forwarded to external advertising or profiling networks.
• Rate-Limiting & Guardrails: Authentication endpoints enforce bounded attempt
windows per process, and TTS synthesis restricts input lengths to prevent abuse.

10. Technical Requirements (HW/SW)
Hardware Requirements:
Development & Server Machine: Computer with an Intel Core i5 processor (or equivalent
multi-core AMD / Apple Silicon chip).
RAM: Minimum 8 GB RAM (16 GB recommended for concurrent Next.js compiler, Node.js
API runtime, and browser testing).
Storage: Minimum 2 GB of free disk space for project source code, node_modules
dependencies, runtime accounts store, build artifacts, and local vector index.
Client Access Device: Accessible on any standard personal laptop, desktop, tablet, or
smartphone equipped with a modern web browser (no mobile app download required).
Network / Connectivity: Active internet connection for geospatial map tiles, satellite fire
anomaly telemetry (NASA FIRMS), atmospheric data ingestion, and font/asset delivery.
Software Requirements:
Runtime Environment: Node.js (version 22+ LTS or Node.js 24) and npm workspaces for
dependency and monorepo orchestration.
Backend Stack: Node.js HTTP API service with TypeScript, native environment variable
validation (dotenv), cryptographic security (scrypt, SHA-256 digests), and modular adapter
contracts.
Speech Service Bridge (Optional): Python 3.10+ runtime with FastAPI, Uvicorn, and edge-tts
for multi-modal text-to-speech synthesis.
Frontend Framework: Next.js 16 (App Router), React 19, TypeScript, and Tailwind CSS for
responsive styling and component architecture.
Data & Knowledge Persistence: Atomic file-locked JSON account store (accounts.json), pre-
indexed markdown document knowledge base (frontend/content/*.md), and TypeScript-typed
fixture envelopes.

13. Conclusion
In conclusion, VayuRakshak AI demonstrates that a low-overhead, software-driven web
platform can successfully transform passive air pollution awareness into proactive,
accountable, and explainable personal air defense. By bringing together a modern Next.js 16
and React 19 frontend, a modular Node.js API backend, and TypeScript data contracts, the
platform bridges the gap between atmospheric hazard monitoring and everyday preventive
decision-making under the foundational ethos “We don’t just inform. We protect.”
The system replaces passive AQI notifications with quantifiable, human-in-the-loop
interventions:
• An explainable Indoor Protection Risk Score (0–100) that breaks down vulnerability
into transparent factors rather than an opaque black box.
• An intelligent commute exposure router that computes exposure deltas and
delivers 28% to 43% lower estimated exposure on cleaner travel alternatives.
• Calendar Guard and Protection Autopilot workflows that reschedule outdoor activities
away from hazardous inversion peaks and automate pre-emptive air purifier runs with
single-click approvals, pause controls, and immutable audit logs.
• An interactive assistant (Vayu Guide) providing locally grounded, non-diagnostic
respiratory health advice backed by text-to-speech (TTS) narration.
The end-to-end test scenarios confirmed that all protective workflows operate seamlessly with
high responsiveness and robust session security (salted scrypt hashing and HttpOnly cookies).
While developed as an academic prototype rather than a production citywide IoT grid, the
project proves that actionable exposure reduction and home protection can be achieved
without requiring expensive proprietary hardware. VayuRakshak AI establishes a strong,
scalable foundation for future smart city health defense systems, actively advancing SDG 3
(Good Health & Well-being), SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities & Communities), and SDG 13
(Climate Action).
